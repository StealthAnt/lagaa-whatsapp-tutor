# Learning platform review for Lagaa

Reviewed 15 October 2026. This is a review of platforms named or discovered during Lagaa planning, not every education website on the internet. “Strength” describes the platform's role for a Ghana SHS learner. Claims made by the platform itself are marked as claims, not verified guarantees.

## Strongest sources for Lagaa's core job

| Rank for Lagaa | Platform | Strong points | Gaps / cautions | How Lagaa should use it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ghana Ministry curriculum microsite and NaCCA | Best fit for current Ghana SHS subject/year alignment; learner and teacher materials are grouped by year, subject, section, and curriculum standards. | Mostly a resource library, not a conversational tutor or adaptive practice engine. Some pages are long PDFs; teacher resources should not be passed off as student handouts. | Use as the curriculum map and first source for subject availability and section titles. Link or cite exact sources; explain the ideas in original, short WhatsApp messages. |
| 2 | WAEC Ghana: Chief Examiners' Reports and timetables | Most locally authoritative exam guidance; reports surface subject-specific strengths, common errors, and advice. Timetables support current exam dates. | Reports are not a complete past-question bank; timetable pages do not teach. | Use reports to improve original explanations and misconception feedback. Use the timetable only for dates. |
| 3 | WAEC e-Learning | WAEC-hosted subject pages show selected past questions with examiner observations/remedies; examples include school and private candidate sittings. | Coverage varies by subject/year. The portal is regional and is not verified as a complete Ghana-specific 1993-to-date archive. | Link to a specific original question page when verified; identify its country/sitting/year and never claim the whole bank is complete. |
| 4 | LMS Ghana | Ghana's curriculum microsite hosts a platform guide showing subject/year/paper navigation and a practice area. Potentially the closest local learner platform for mixing materials and practice. | The guide demonstrates only examples; live archive completeness, current access, account requirements, and all-subject availability could not be confirmed. | Offer as an optional external destination only after checking the particular subject and access requirements. Do not ask learners for details to use Lagaa. |

## Useful learning-design models, with adaptation needed

| Platform | Strengths to learn from | Gaps for Lagaa's audience |
|---|---|---|
| Khan Academy | Structured learning paths, self-paced practice, exercise feedback, and skill mastery. | Its course sequence is not the Ghana SHS curriculum or WASSCE syllabus; videos and platform navigation add data use. Borrow the mastery loop, not its content/sequence. |
| PhET | Interactive simulations make invisible science and math relationships observable; it offers teaching activities and downloadable/offline simulations. | Primarily math and science, needs a screen/device, and is less useful in a text-only WhatsApp exchange. Lagaa should suggest a simulation only when it materially helps and give a text alternative. |
| CK-12 / Flexi | Digital explanations, practice, and an AI tutor in one learning environment. | US-oriented materials may not map to Ghana SHS. Its privacy notice says it may collect account details and interaction/device data; Lagaa should not send personal learner data to external tools. |
| Class CrunchLabs | Strong inquiry, design-build-test cycles, hands-on projects, and real-world engineering examples. | Its curriculum is aligned to US state/NGSS standards and Grades 6–8, below much of Lagaa's SHS scope. Use only as optional project inspiration after adapting difficulty, Ghana context, safety, and curriculum fit. |
| BBC Bitesize | Short, clear revision resources and broad subject coverage. | UK curriculum and exam-board assumptions can differ from Ghana's; use only for a specific concept after checking fit, not as a WASSCE authority. |

## Main gaps across the market

1. No reviewed platform combines Ghana curriculum sections, all-subject tutoring, WAEC-style practice, and conversational explanations in a WhatsApp-first flow.
2. Past-question coverage is fragmented. “1993 to date” is a platform claim, not proof that every subject, sitting, paper, and year is present or correct.
3. Sources blur together: official curriculum, actual past questions, examiner commentary, third-party scans, and newly written mocks are easy to confuse.
4. Feedback often ends at a score or answer key. Students need the reason an option is wrong, a hint at the right level, and a next question that responds to their understanding.
5. Several tools require accounts, phone numbers, school names, leaderboards, or ad/analytics tracking. That conflicts with Lagaa's decision not to ask learners for personal details or build a student profile.
6. Interactive simulations and video lessons can teach well but cost more data and are not available inside a text-only WhatsApp conversation.
7. A real exam timer is missing in Lagaa's current WhatsApp adapter: it cannot see app switching, reliably enforce a clock, or promise an automatic time-up notification.

## Clean-room decisions for Lagaa

- Use NaCCA and the Ministry's Year 1–3 resources as the source of subject and section titles; use WAEC Ghana for local exam guidance; use WAEC e-Learning for selected authentic exam examples. Show source, year, sitting, and official/independent status when known.
- Keep the learner's chosen subject, year, current mode, and quiz answers only in the conversation. Do not request a name, age, school, phone, index number, programme, or account. Do not introduce leaderboards, analytics profiles, streaks, or external AI accounts.
- Offer Learn, Objective Quiz (maximum 40 questions), and Full WASSCE-style Mock (objective plus subjective sections). Let learners switch or stop whenever they choose. A mock's counts, marks, and timing must come from a verified subject format or be labelled approximate.
- Teach with a short cycle: explain, worked example, one retrieval check, kind feedback, then a hint or next step. Adjust difficulty from the learner's answers within the current chat; do not claim long-term adaptive tracking.
- Keep messages text-first and small. Offer optional external video/simulation links only when relevant, with a low-data/text alternative. Adapt hands-on STEAM tasks to Ghanaian contexts and screen for hazards.
- Write original explanations, practice questions, examples, and distractors. Do not copy platform question banks or imply generated questions are actual past papers. Link students to past-paper source pages instead.
- Explain that timed mode's clock continues while WhatsApp is closed. Until persistent timers and scheduled WhatsApp delivery exist, call it an advisory timer and never promise app-away detection or an automatic alert.

## Loophole audit and current implementation boundary

“Covered” here means identified and given a product rule or an explicit limitation; it does not mean every risk is technically eliminated. This is a prototype with an AI model, WhatsApp, and hosting provider in the message path.

| Risk or loophole | Current rule / mitigation | What remains before production |
|---|---|---|
| Learner is asked for identifying information | System instructions prohibit asking for personal details, credentials, or sensitive traits; WhatsApp's routing identity is disclosed. | Runtime redaction is not implemented. Provider-side logging, retention, access, and deletion settings need verification and a documented privacy notice. |
| Learner or uploaded source tries to override safety rules | Instructions treat pasted/retrieved documents as untrusted study material and set precedence for safety/privacy and source rights. | Prompt injection is not technically filtered; model-level rules can fail. Avoid web/file tools and external actions unless separately reviewed and constrained. |
| Quiz exceeds 40, repeats, or uses cap loopholes | The prompt counts every item and disallows splitting a single quiz across sessions. | No structured quiz state or runtime counter exists; the model can still violate the rule. Add a quiz state machine/counter and test it before relying on the limit. |
| Correct answer pattern or implausible distractors | Four parallel options, plausible misconceptions, randomized order, and varying correct letters are specified. | Randomization and one-correct-answer validation are not code-enforced. Add item schema validation and answer-key checks; qualitative “closeness” percentages cannot be guaranteed for every subject. |
| False claim of real WASSCE question, coverage, timing, or official alignment | Require provenance, year/sitting labels, official vs independent status, and explicit uncertainty; mocks are labeled original. | Source coverage and copyright permission are unverified across many third-party archives. The prompt cannot independently verify a link or answer key. |
| Student assumes an actual stopwatch or app-away detector | Chat-based advisory timing only; no app-switching detection or promised push at expiry. | Requires a durable clock, consented session state, scheduled delivery, and WhatsApp policy review. Current channel lacks these capabilities. |
| Dangerous hands-on project or immediate crisis | Safety-screen activities; redirect hazardous builds; urgent risk responses point to trusted adults/local services. | This is not a safeguarding or emergency-response system. Establish locally appropriate escalation and adult-support policy before a minor-focused pilot. |
| Platform account or external AI exposes learner data | No account/profile/streak/leaderboard and no forwarding of messages to other study platforms. | The configured Google, Meta, and Vercel services still process data; review their current terms, location/retention controls, and applicable Ghanaian data-protection obligations. |
| Confusing full mock with 40-item objective quiz | 40 applies to Objective Quiz; Full WASSCE-style Mock is separate and follows verified subject format. | Model may conflate modes; add explicit mode/question state if exact behavior is required. |

The repo's `agent/instructions.md` and curriculum skill now state these rules. They are model instructions, not hard-coded enforcement. The README describes this limitation so that prompt guidance is not mistaken for a guarantee. The current implementation does not yet include a deterministic quiz engine, PII detection/redaction, moderation layer, source verification service, persistent timer, or production retention controls.
